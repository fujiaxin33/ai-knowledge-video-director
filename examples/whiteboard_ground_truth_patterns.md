# Abstract Whiteboard Ground Truth

This library stores transferable production evidence, not episode scripts, settings, character names, timecodes, or reusable storyboards.

## Positive patterns

### Clean speech before motion

Lock one coherent A-roll before timing drawing, captions, characters, and SFX. Preserve intentional reveal, reaction, comedy, and knowledge holds. A later speech cut invalidates downstream timing until it is retimed.

### Nested progressive construction

Build a scene through semantic levels: environment → object → character → action → relationship. Each addition changes meaning; the audience sees the scene form instead of receiving complete images in sequence.

### Action and reaction

A character is useful when its state changes. A clear action followed by a readable reaction can carry both personality and explanation.

### Knowledge Hero

One short handwritten relationship, generous empty space, restrained emphasis, and an isolated final hold can lock the lesson more effectively than another busy page.

### Exact event timing

Hand, stroke/mask front, visible impact, reaction, caption, and SFX each bind to explicit timeline events. Review final-canvas pixels rather than assumed source coordinates.

## Negative patterns

### Static page sequence

Complete illustrated pages revealed one after another produce presentation rhythm even when their art style looks hand-drawn.

### Persistent stale asset

A background, character, label, or prop that survives beyond its narrative job blocks later content and weakens semantic timing.

### Incomplete silhouette or frame

Cropping a character, speech box, label, or border damages readability. Validate the full visible contour and inner padding at final 1920×1080 or the actual delivery canvas.

### Fake handwriting

A moving Hand cannot legitimize an independently fading asset. The tip must agree with the current stroke endpoint or reveal front.

### Transcript-like caption

Long subtitles or duplicated spoken copy become the primary visual and compete with drawing. Compress to one semantic line when Whiteboard mode is selected.

### UI residue

Generic cards, buttons, dashboards, and boxed option panels make a whiteboard story feel like product UI. Use drawing, physical choices, bubbles, and character interaction unless real UI is the subject.

## Boundary

These patterns guide planning and QC. They do not prescribe a particular topic, shop, mountain, monk, character, joke, or ending. New projects must invent their own story shell.
