# Visual Lifecycle and Scale

## Object lifecycle

Every meaningful object has:

`DRAW/ENTRY -> ACTION -> REACTION -> HOLD IF NEEDED -> EXIT / ERASE / TRANSFORM`

Objects without a current teaching or comedy job are residue. Clear them before they become a background wall.

## Scale hierarchy

- Primary story character/action: roughly 40–65% of the effective visual area.
- Knowledge hero: roughly 50–70% when used.
- Comedy impact: may reach 70–90% briefly.
- Supporting objects: few, legible, and visibly subordinate.

Supporting elements must not remain smaller than captions. When that occurs, remove supports or enlarge the active object before enlarging text.

Avoid meaningless large-small-large oscillation. Do not shrink characters into 10–15% icons while captions or cards dominate.

## Static card/image risk

Risk rises when a complete image appears between adjacent frames without Draw, Mask, Build, Transform, or intentional Impact; when the same composition holds without meaningful action; or when the canvas repeatedly resets as pages. A high-quality still is not automatically a well-directed scene.

## Adaptive captions

Caption ON for ordinary or dense knowledge that the picture cannot fully carry. Caption OFF for pure reaction, meme impact, central complete handwriting, literal evidence already readable, clean hero, or ending question. Keep captions single-line when possible, within about 55–65% frame width and 4–5.5% frame height, in the lower safe area, and subordinate to the primary action.

## Callback duration

A callback reminds; it does not reteach. Use the minimum recognizable actor plus two or three evidence actions, then exit. Target roughly 2–6 seconds; fail review when the callback occupies a new teaching section.
