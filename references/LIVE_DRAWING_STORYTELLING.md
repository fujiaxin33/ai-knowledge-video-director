# Live Drawing Storytelling Layer

Use this composable layer when the viewer should experience the explanation being discovered on one living canvas. It normally pairs with `WHITEBOARD_STORYTELLING`, and may also annotate a Standard screen beat. It is not a new primary mode.

## Core priority

`DRAW WHILE TALKING` has higher priority than merely `DRAW BEFORE SHOW`.

The final picture must not exist at the start. Voice and drawing jointly create it:

`spoken cue -> Hand action -> visible change -> meaning residue -> next cue`

The Hand is an actor. It may point, hesitate, circle, underline, compare, connect, erase, move an object, reveal a face, or deliver a comedy impact. Decorative Hand motion fails.

## Hand-tip contract

- Hand tip must track the active stroke or mask front within the declared tolerance.
- Reveal must not continue after the Hand stops unless the plan names a separate autonomous effect.
- A nearby Hand plus independent fade is fake handwriting.
- Important keywords should land on or immediately after their spoken cue, not several seconds early.

## Continuous canvas

Prefer one evolving knowledge map over page replacement. Reuse position and residue:

- keep only the minimum prior object needed to understand the next relation;
- erase or transform stale objects;
- move the viewport only when it follows the Hand or reveals anticipated empty space;
- avoid full-canvas reset followed by a complete new layout.

Two consecutive page-like resets require explicit justification. Five consecutive `Clear Canvas -> New Frame` beats are a HIGH page-turn risk. Independent examples should reset rather than accumulate, but the reset must be directed as Erase/Transform/Viewport movement instead of a completed-slide swap.

## Anticipatory empty space and curiosity

Reserve visible empty space before the next idea arrives. The composition should make the viewer wonder what will fill it within about 0.5–2 seconds. Seventy percent empty space is valid when the Hand is about to enter; the same empty frame with a static character for five seconds fails. In each roughly 10–20 second story segment, prefer at least one incomplete object, unexpected drawing, progressive reveal, or transformation. A live-drawing beat needs at least two meaningful becoming states; a final-state-only storyboard does not prove live drawing.

## Knowledge as residue

Prefer a compact trail left by action over repeated title cards. A Knowledge Hero is optional, not mandatory. Use it only when a clean reduction materially improves retention; hold briefly, then continue or clear.

Progressive build is valid only for one semantic scene, location, object hierarchy, or story chain such as `mountain -> temple -> monk`. Separate examples must reset; do not preserve unrelated objects merely to claim a continuous canvas. A concluding knowledge map may miniaturize, fade, or reduce old objects while retaining only useful relations.

## Asset routing

- Simple explanatory objects: true draw or simple programmatic strokes.
- High-quality comedy characters/scenes: mask draw, assembly build, or Hand reveal to the approved final asset.
- Real screen evidence: do not redraw it as fake UI; freeze and annotate the real screen.
- Sudden impact: intentional pop is allowed only when the pop is the joke or result.

Simple drawing can be expressive and fast, but it must not be an unfinished placeholder.

## Handwriting transitions

Use the last stroke, underline, circle, or object as a bridge: a circle becomes a lens, a line becomes a road, a note becomes evidence, or an avatar contracts into a semantic point. Prefer `ERASE -> DRAW`, `OBJECT TRANSFORM`, or viewport follow over a slide cut.

Camera/viewport may pan with the Hand across a canvas larger than the delivery frame. Do not re-center every new object or add decorative zooms; viewport motion follows drawing or knowledge focus.

## Live-drawing comedy

Comedy is performed, not displayed. The Hand can try the wrong object, pause, correct it, stamp a result, reveal an unexpected character, or erase a failed attempt. Preserve anticipation and reaction; drawing speed may accelerate setup but not the punch hold.

## Gate questions

Before render, answer:

1. What is absent at the start and visibly becomes true?
2. Which spoken cue controls each important stroke or reveal?
3. What does the Hand do as an actor?
4. What residue remains, and why?
5. How does this scene transition without a page turn?
6. Is the final asset quality preserved?
7. Does any reveal continue after the Hand stops?
8. Could the same meaning be clearer with fewer objects and larger action?
